OCR v5: scanner-style Bangla + English text reading for the SARA glass

A rebuilt text-reading mode for a Raspberry Pi 5 smart glass: it finds and flattens the page, finds every text line with PP-OCRv5, and reads each line with a new 2.3 M-parameter Bangla + English line recogniser trained for the glass camera. It runs fully offline. On photographed pages it makes about 17 times fewer errors than the previous reader (0.99 % vs 17.2 % character error) and is 8 to 15 times faster; on isolated scene words, Bangla got worse. Both results are reported below.

1Summary of results

All numbers are character error rate (CER): the share of reference characters that would have to be changed, inserted or deleted to turn the reading into the reference text. Lower is better; 2 % CER means about 98 % of characters are right. "v4" is the glass's reader before this work (EasyOCR with a fine-tuned recogniser, CRAFT detector); "v5" is this work.

Mixed result, stated plainly. v5 is far better on its target task (photographed pages and labels) and on English scene words, and 8–15× faster everywhere. It is worse than v4 on isolated Bangla scene words (32.9 % vs 21.1 % CER) and on the short rendered test phrases (3.5 % vs 1.4 %; Bangla 5.6 % vs 1.5 %). Section 11 lists what causes this and what would fix it.

2Problem and goal

Text reading was the weakest mode of the glass. On rendered text v4 was accurate (1.4 % test CER), but on real camera input it was not: 20–34 % CER on a real glass-camera book page (benchmark of 24 Nov 2026), 29.6 % on real photographed words, 17.2 % on the camera-page set of this report, and 3.7–13 s per capture on the CPU. The goal set for this work:

  • Accurate on what the glass camera actually sees: pages and labels at an angle, shadows, blur, low resolution.
  • Fast: a page read in about a second, the first line spoken while the rest is still being read.
  • Lite and offline: Raspberry Pi 5 (8 GB) with the Hailo-8 AI HAT (26 TOPS), no internet, no new heavy dependency.
  • Like a scanner app: read by itself when text is steady in view, with spoken hints ("bring it closer", "hold still").

Four-point warp (homography) without a visible page edge

When a page is held close it fills or leaves the picture, so no page outline exists. The page's four corners are then estimated from the main text block itself: the top edge is the line through the top corners of the first text line, the bottom edge the line through the bottom corners of the last, the left edge a robust (Huber) line fitted through the left ends of all lines, and the right edge one fitted through the right ends of the full-length lines (ragged short lines left out). Each edge is then moved outwards until every line end lies inside, and the corners are pushed out by 0.6 line heights. The four intersections give the quadrilateral; a homography (cv2.getPerspectiveTransform) maps it to an upright rectangle. It is applied when the larger of the top-edge tilt and the keystone angle between opposite edges exceeds 1.5°, and the quadrilateral must be convex with corner angles of 55–125°. In the tests, lines come out level to within 0.01°.

Enhancement filters

Scanner-style filters were tried both on the picture the text finder looks at and on the picture the lines are read from: shadow removal (divide by a morphological estimate of the paper, then stretch), CLAHE, unsharp masking and bilateral denoising, alone and combined. Only shadow removal for the finder helped, and only on document-like pictures. Filters on the reader's picture did not help, because the recogniser was trained on natural camera pictures. See section 7.

CTC decoding

The recogniser outputs, for every 4 input columns, a probability over 236 characters plus a blank. Greedy CTC decoding takes the most likely class at each step, merges repeats and drops blanks. A line's confidence is the mean probability of its kept characters. Bangla is encoded as Unicode code points (NFC), so a pre-base vowel sign such as ি is emitted after its consonant even though it is drawn before it. The 1×7 context convolutions give each step enough view of its neighbours for that.

4Recogniser model

A convolution-only text-line recogniser, built for this project. Its blocks are inverted residuals (MobileNetV2 style) that shrink the height 48 → 1 while keeping a quarter of the width, then four context blocks (depthwise 1×7 + feed-forward) along the line, and a 1×1 CTC head. It has no LSTM and no attention, so it exports cleanly to ONNX, quantises to 8 bits, and is the kind of network Hailo's Dataflow Compiler accepts at a fixed width (for example 48×320) for a later move onto the AI HAT.

LayerOutput (C×H×W, input 1×48×320)Parameters

Character set: 236 characters (Bangla letters, vowel signs, hasanta, nukta, khanda ta, Bangla digits, ৳, । ॥; printable ASCII; Latin-1 accented letters; ° • € £) plus the CTC blank. Labels are NFC-normalised; typographic quotes and dashes become ASCII.

8Confusion analysis

For OCR, the confusion matrix is built per character: each reading is aligned to its reference with the edit-distance alignment. Each reference character (row) is then read as some character (column), or missed (column ∅). The diagonal holds correct reads. The 14 most frequent characters of each script are shown; "other" collects reads as any character outside them.

11Limitations and next steps

  • Not yet measured on the glass. The camera-page set is rendered and realistic, but not real photographs. Next: run scripts/ocr5_try.py on the laptop webcam, then on the Pi with the glass-camera page set (~/Desktop/glass_ocr/glass_set, 50 shots of 14 pages) and time it with the AI HAT.
  • Isolated Bangla scene words (32.9 % vs 21.1 %): mostly words not found or conjuncts missed on stylised shop-sign lettering. More real Bangla word photos are the main lever; there are only 2,989 in training. Bangladeshi sign and label photos from the glass itself would help most.
  • Short rendered phrases (3.5 % vs 1.4 %; Bangla 5.6 %): partly Tiro Bangla and Noto Sans Bengali, test fonts unlike any training font. Adding more Bangla font families to training (Kalpurush, SolaimanLipi, Nikosh, where licences allow) is the cheap fix.
  • Recogniser on the AI HAT. The network is convolution-only so it can be compiled with Hailo's Dataflow Compiler (needs an x86 Linux PC, not available here). That would leave the Pi's CPU almost free during reading.
  • 8-bit model loses 1.8 points on Bangla; a quantisation-aware fine-tune would likely close that.
  • Handwriting is not covered by v5 (the Ekush letter reader of v4 remains in the code).
