SARA · Smart Assistive Reading Aid · Project report A–Z

SARA: an offline Bangla smart glass for blind and low-vision people

A wearable glass on a Raspberry Pi 5 (8 GB) with a Hailo-8 AI HAT that reads Bangla and English text, names objects, recognises and counts Bangladeshi Taka notes and screens them for counterfeits without ever accusing a note, describes facial expressions, and speaks everything offline in Bangla. This report summarises every part: hardware, algorithms, models, data, results and limits.

1Problem and goal

In Bangladesh a blind person usually has to ask someone which note they were given and whether it is real, what a medicine label or sign says, and what or who is in front of them. Commercial reading glasses (e.g. OrCam) do not support Bangla or Taka and are expensive; research glasses send pictures to a server. Goal: one wearable that does these jobs offline, in Bangla, fast enough to use, on a Raspberry Pi 5, and that never accuses a genuine note of being fake.

4Text reading (OCR v5)

The earlier reader (v4: CRAFT detector + fine-tuned EasyOCR) was accurate on rendered text but poor on real camera pictures (17–40 % CER) and slow (13 s per page). OCR v5 replaces it with a scanner-style pipeline:

Auto captureFires when text is in view, the picture has been steady 0.5 s (thumbnail difference < 4 levels) and sharp (Laplacian variance ≥ 70 % of the recent best); hints "bring it closer" / "hold still".
Find linesPP-OCRv5 mobile DBNet (Hailo HEF or ONNX CPU) → probability map → DB post-process (thresh 0.3, box 0.5, unclip 1.6). Document-like pictures also searched shadow-free.
Four-point warpPage outline → homography to a rectangle. No outline → corners estimated from the text lines (top, bottom, Huber-fitted margins), warp when tilt or keystone > 1.5°.
Cut linesEach line warped out along its own tilt, scaled to 48 px height.
Read linesSARA recogniser (conv + CTC, greedy decoding); each line also read sharpened and contrast-boosted, most confident reading kept; 180° retry for upside-down lines.
Order and speakMain text block (scenes), rows top-to-bottom; first page line spoken while the rest is read.

SARA line recogniser

  • Network: 2.26 M parameters, convolution only (MobileNetV2-style inverted residuals reduce 48 → 1 rows at ¼ width, 4 depthwise 1×7 context blocks, 1×1 CTC head); 236 characters + blank (Bangla letters, signs, digits, ৳, ASCII, Latin-1). ONNX 9.0 MB (8-bit 2.5 MB).
  • Training data: ~6 M rendered lines drawn on the fly (1.5 M Bangla Wikipedia sentences, 50 k-word lists, numbers/prices/medicine labels, mixed scripts), shaped with HarfBuzz in 15 Bangla and 224 English training fonts, passed through a C270 camera model (9–42 px text, defocus, motion blur, noise, JPEG, shadows, tilt, perspective); plus 52,867 real ICDAR-2019-MLT training words drawn half Bangla, half Latin.
  • Training: CTC loss, AdamW, one-cycle lr 2e-3, mixed precision, batches of fixed width buckets; 9 k + 80 k steps (~3.5 h on an RTX 4060); checkpoint chosen on validation.

Scanner settings (chosen on validation)

Filters on the picture the reader sees (whitening, CLAHE, sharpening) did not help as plain pre-processing; shadow removal helped only the text finder and only on documents (it hid light-on-dark sign text). Reading each line three ways and keeping the most confident reading helped real words. A colour "scan" (denoised, white-balanced, sharpened) is made only for display.

5Taka notes and counterfeit screening

  • Zero-annotation detector: 5,073 BanglaTaka note photos pasted onto COCO backgrounds (perspective, rotation, scale 25–90 %, colour, blur, noise, JPEG; 10 % note-free) → 22,333 composites with automatic YOLO labels; YOLOv8s, 80 epochs.
  • Hand-held fine-tune: 6,000 synthetic close-ups (warped, folded, fingers, blur); epoch and threshold chosen on NSTU validation photos. Announce threshold 0.75 (open-set rule, rejects unknown notes such as 1 Taka). A second detector looks again when the first is unsure (cascade).
  • Several notes: sure boxes, one per physical note (overlap > 0.5 = same note), spoken with count and total. Live mode follows each note over the scans: a note is named once it reaches 0.75, or when the same value leads in 4 of its last 5 scans at mean confidence ≥ 0.5 with no close rival; a swapped note loses its old name.
  • Multi-view counterfeit check (JaalTaka): prefix training (random 1–6 views during training) fixes the collapse of fusion networks when fewer views are captured (1 view: 71.2 % → 98.2 %).
  • Serial-number audit: 68 of 87 test counterfeits on the standard split share a serial with training counterfeits; a print-disjoint split (no shared prints) is used for honest results.
  • DMWR watermark check: lamp removed by a quotient image I / (Gσ∗I), compared with a per-value genuine template; two tiny CNN streams + 4 statistics (47,818 parameters).
  • Safe verdict: "সম্ভবত আসল" (likely genuine) only if p > τ = 0.99959 (highest validation counterfeit score; conformal bound 1/(n+1) = 1.1 %), otherwise "আসল কিনা হাতে যাচাই করুন" (check by hand). "জাল" (fake) is never spoken. The SARA Trust Index adds image quality and pose to the message.
  • UV fibre check (ENFR): fluorescence = light the lamp colour does not explain, E = max(G − aB, R − bB), top-hat filtered and normalised; thin fibres 5–70 px counted.

6Objects, emotion and voice

  • Objects: YOLOv8s on COCO (80 classes), confidence 0.5, at most 3 objects per scan; on the AI HAT with Hailo's compile.
  • Emotion: YuNet face detector + EfficientNet-V2-S trained on RAF-DB (7 expressions); also adapts speech ("ঠিক আছে। ধীরে শুনুন।").
  • Speech: Piper Bangla and English voices; mixed lines are split by script and spoken with each voice; queue clearing on mode change; first page line spoken early.
  • Voice commands: Vosk small English, closed grammar, wake word "glass", deaf while speaking; chosen among 138 designs on 8 synthetic voices, tested on 12 held-out voices.

9Evaluation method

  • Splits disjoint by physical unit (note, print, writer, font, phrase).
  • Every setting chosen on validation data; test sets read after the choice. Trained models repeated with seeds 42/43/44 where reported.
  • Paired comparisons with McNemar tests; proportions with Wilson 95 % intervals.
  • Text: character error rate (edit distance / reference length, Unicode NFC). Taka: correct value; objects: COCO mAP; emotion: accuracy and macro-F1.
  • All claims traced to result files (claim registry); 107 of 136 claims value-checked, corrections logged.

12What did not work

  • A direct genuine/counterfeit verdict on whole notes (wrong on 20–59 % of genuine notes): switched off and replaced by the safe verdict.
  • Learned view-selection policies, simulated federated training, occlusion (55 % covered → 51.9 %).
  • OCR: binarisation, Tesseract, TrOCR, PaddleOCR (no Bangla), dictionaries / KenLM / ByT5 correction, multi-scale; plain clean-up filters before the recogniser; shadow removal on sign photos.
  • Voice: VAD end-of-speech cut commands; Bangla offline commands only 45–62 %.
  • UV v1 was fooled by JPEG colour smear (fixed: 92.8 % → honest 85.6 %).

13Limitations and next steps

  • Glass camera: most figures come from datasets or a laptop webcam; the C270 is fixed-focus. A camera with autofocus (Pi Camera Module 3) would help text and notes most.
  • Taka on webcam: live confidence drops for some notes (5, 50, 100, 1,000 Taka); fine-tuning the detector on webcam-style pictures is the next step.
  • Text: isolated stylised Bangla shop-sign words remain weaker than v4; more real Bangla photos and Bangladeshi fonts are needed. OCR v5 timing on the Pi still to be measured; the recogniser can be compiled for the Hailo-8.
  • Counterfeit evidence: about 24 unseen prints and about 4 physical counterfeit notes; the UV check is untested on real counterfeits.
  • Users: no completed user study with blind participants yet (protocol ready).
